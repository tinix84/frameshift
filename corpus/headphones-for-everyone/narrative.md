# Headphones for everyone

## Request as it arrived

“Buy the team noise-cancelling headphones.”

## Why it misleads

The request proposes a purchase for a noise problem, but it leaves out the
synthetic meeting-scheduling decision that creates the interruption and who is
accountable for that shared working condition. The requester may not own that
decision.

## Reframe

Surface the absent meeting-scheduling owner before proposing a frame the
requester cannot act on. The immediate reframe is an ownership question:
identify who can set or change the scheduling practice and invite that person
to state the outcome they own.

## Downstream change

The request remains a proposal, and no headphones are approved. A human
checkpoint must identify the accountable owner and the affected people before
the system compares schedule, workspace, or equipment options.
