# Elevator wait complaint

## Request as it arrived

“Make the elevators faster.”

## Why it misleads

The request assumes elapsed travel or cycle time is the problem. The complaint
is about waiting as experienced by people, so optimising car speed may miss
dispatching, arrival information, crowding, or the distribution of waits.

## Reframe

Reframe the request around perceived wait: define whose waiting experience is
being measured, how it is observed, and what acceptable experience means. The
result changes the measure and the evidence plan before it chooses a building
or elevator intervention.

## Downstream change

The owner must confirm the target population, the current and desired perceived
wait, and how reported experience relates to elapsed wait. Only then should
causal hypotheses and interventions be compared. No speed improvement is
approved by this result.
