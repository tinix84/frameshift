# Battery cost structure

## Request as it arrived

“Qualify a cheaper cell supplier.”

## Why it misleads

The request names a component-level purchasing action. It treats the supplier
as the problem before the product cost target, cost drivers, and accountable
owner are known. A lower cell price could leave the product cost structure
unchanged if other drivers dominate.

## Reframe

Reframe the request from qualifying one cheaper cell supplier to understanding
the product cost structure and the cost target it must move. The ladder moves
from component to product. A synthetic cost-target cause-and-effect
decomposition can connect the target to candidate processes and cost factors,
then rank candidate drivers by financial impact. This is a method for the case,
not a claim that the cited paper establishes an Ishikawa taxonomy or any
battery-specific driver.

## Downstream change

The next work is to name the product cost outcome, identify its accountable
owner, and establish the current cost structure. Supplier qualification is one
possible later intervention among alternatives such as bill-of-materials
architecture, design-to-cost, or platform reuse. No option is approved by this
result.
